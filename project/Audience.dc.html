still · who it’s for

For people who notice things.

Casual iPhone shooters who stop for a palm against glass, late light on a wall, a nice table, and then get a photo that looks like nothing. They don’t want to learn photography. They want this shot to work, and over time, to trust their own eye.

Primary

The noticer

“I stopped because the light was doing something. The photo didn’t get any of it.”
Today
  • Shoots on the built-in Camera, never touches settings
  • Takes ten near-identical shots and hopes one works
  • Crops and filters later to rescue it
Wants
  • The photo to look like it felt
  • To be told what to do, right now, in normal words
  • An eye for it, without taking a class
Worries
  • Looking clueless, phone up, in public
  • Missing the moment while fiddling
  • Being graded, or having photos uploaded

Drawn from the brief plus our own assumptions, not interviews yet. Check it against 6–8 short conversations with people who fit before v1 locks.

Three moments it has to work in

On a walk

Ten seconds, one hand, friends waiting. Streets, buildings, trees.

So: a tip within a second, and the shutter always works.

Somewhere new

On a trip, no signal, roaming off. Beaches, landscapes, landmarks.

So: fully offline, no account, nothing to sign into.

One of a friend

Someone posing and losing patience. Casual portraits, café tables, food.

So: help place people, never rate faces.

Why they’d switch, and what holds them back

Switching happens when push + pull beat worry + habit
Push · away from today

“My photos look flat and I can’t say why.”

Answer: the after-shot sheet names what changed, in plain words.

Pull · toward still

“Someone next to me saying ‘tilt up a bit.’”

Answer: one tip and one arrow, updated within about a second.

Worry · holds them back

“Will it judge me, slow me down, or upload my stuff?”

Answer: no scores, no red, everything on the phone, shutter never locked.

Habit · holds them back

“Camera is one swipe from my Lock Screen.”

Answer: open from the Lock Screen or Camera Control, straight to the viewfinder.

Not for

Enthusiasts who want dials

Manual exposure, RAW, histograms. Pro camera apps already do this well. still won’t add controls to win them.

Creators chasing reach

No “will this do well” predictions or engagement scores. The promise is better-looking, not better-performing.

Fix-it-later editors

No filters, retouching or AI edits. still helps before the shutter, not after.
