Six rules the interface follows.

Each one comes from the audience on the left. Each one shows up somewhere in the flow below.

Open
~1s · first tip
01

Seconds, not settings

Because they’re mid-walk with someone waiting.

Opens to the viewfinder. First idea in about a second. The shutter is always live.

Point up toward the palms.
02

One thing at a time

Because two instructions at once is how people freeze.

One tip, one arrow, one subject. Ideas wait in a row of three below the photo, never on top of it.

03

Show, then say

Because in sun and on the move, nobody reads.

Put the amber dot on the white spot. The frame closes in as you get near. Words are the backup.

never red
04

Coach, never judge

Because being graded is the worry that stops them.

No scores, no red. White until it’s green. Shooting off-target gets “Saved. Your call.”

Nothing stands out yet.
05

Honest when unsure

Because one wrong tip costs more trust than no tip.

When nothing stands out it says so and asks you to point at one thing, instead of inventing advice.

Because the goal is their eye, not our arrows.

How we’ll know it works

still never phones home, so there are no analytics. We learn in research instead. Pass marks are set before the test.

Sidewalk test

8 casual shooters, a real street, a real iPhone. Pass if 6 of 8 reach “lined up” in under 15 seconds without help.

Side by side

Show each person their start view next to their still shot. Pass if 6 of 8 pick the still shot.

Quiet week

An opt-in photo diary for a week. Pass if half move from Guide to Hints or Quiet on their own.

Test first: that people will actually follow movement tips in public. If they won’t, nothing else here matters.
